Synthetic data can be used to increase the amount of data available for training segmentation models, which in healthcare is often limited by annotation time and patient privacy. A synthetic dataset of brain metastases in MRI has been generated in an earlier master’s thesis, where radiologists could not tell the real and synthetic images apart. How can you do a fair comparison of segmentation models if the data they are trained on are statistically different? In this project I compare 37 624 synthetic 2D slices generated by a DDPM with 3 823 2D slices extracted from real 3D volumes. When looking at the segmentation masks, there are some clear differences. In the synthetic data, 65% of the segments are a single pixel, as opposed to 6% in the real data. These small segments are darker than the brain tissue and have an intensity close to the background, which indicates that they are noise from the generation and not lesions. I propose four strategies for removing them, and an experiment plan for measuring how they change the differences between the datasets and how much data is lost. This project prepares the data that will be used in my master’s thesis for investigating how segmentation models learn from real and synthetic data using XAI.

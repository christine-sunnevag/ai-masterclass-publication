As technologies continue to develop and evolve, so does the discourse surrounding their potential benefits and risks across various separate dimensions, including their environmental and societal impacts. Platforms such as YouTube host a growing amount of long-form content, such as interviews, podcasts, and discussions, where these opinions are expressed. This content offers a valuable source of data, but its format, length and, unstructured nature present challenges for automated analysis.

This paper explores the potential of Natural Language Processing (NLP), specifically Aspect-Based Sentiment Analysis (ABSA), for analysing technology-related discourse in YouTube transcripts, as well as the role Large Language Models (LLMs) might play in it. The study investigates existing approaches to ABSA, with particular attention to the challenges of processing long documents, the limitations of automatically generated transcripts, and the capabilities of LLMs in NLP tasks. The identified literature indicates that existing methodologies are promising for long-documents, but their applicability for opinion extraction from transcribed speech remains uncertain.

The paper establishes a theoretical foundation for a future master's thesis, which will focus on designing, implementing, and evaluating a software pipeline for extraction and classifying aspect-specific opinions from long-form YouTube transcripts. The pipeline will aim to support researchers studying technology-related discourse while enabling the evaluation and comparison of selected NLP approaches.

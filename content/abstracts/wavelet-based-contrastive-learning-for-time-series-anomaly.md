Time series anomaly detection is a widespread task in an increasingly digitalized world, and its performance is highly sensitive to how the data is represented. Recent benchmarking work has also shown that many commonly used datasets contain flawed labels, and that popular metrics such as point-adjusted F1 inflate results. Wavelets describe a signal in both time and frequency, and contrastive learning can learn representations without labels, but contrastive time–wavelet representations have not been evaluated for anomaly detection on a large, reliable and general purpose benchmark. We propose to adapt a contrastive time–wavelet method, originally designed for classification, to unsupervised anomaly detection on the TSB-AD benchmark, and plan to study how wavelet transforms with differentshift behavior affect detection performance, measured with VUS-PR.

Computer science students are usually well-trained in designing algorithms and building systems. However, designing robust experiments and tests requires knowledge that might not be present in the CS curriculum. In this talk, we explore how to apply computational thinking not just to the algorithms we write, but to the entire experimental workflow.

The goal is not to enforce flawless software engineering from day one, but to adopt practical practices that are "good enough" habits that will ensure our project can be assessed by, shared with, and reproduced by others.

The session covers project organization and data management, software and collaboration practices, version control, as well as statistical tests and rigor.

From master's thesis to research articles---no matter the deliverable, this set of skills comes useful regardless of our career level.

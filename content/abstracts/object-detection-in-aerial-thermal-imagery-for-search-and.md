In search and rescue (SAR) operations, thermal cameras on helicopters and drones can reveal persons in darkness and poor visibility, and deep learning-based object detectors can relieve operators of the demanding task of inspecting the video manually.

Yet a detector is most useful if its operators know when to trust it. This paper presents a structured literature review of object detection in aerial thermal imagery, examining which challenges current approaches address and to what extent the uncertainty of their detections is considered. We screened 779 records from Scopus by title and abstract and included 119 primary studies. Most studies use drones, and 83 of the 119 titles name a UAV or drone. Only six titles name high altitude, and none names a helicopter. Only three studies name uncertainty or calibration in their titles, and only one of them calibrates the confidence of the detector and evaluates its calibration. This gap suggests that calibrated confidence, not only accuracy, should be a central concern for thermal detection in SAR, and points to uncertainty estimation as a promising direction for future work.

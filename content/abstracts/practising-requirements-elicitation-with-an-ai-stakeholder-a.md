Requirements Elicitation (RE) is an important part of software engineering that involves gathering technical information from a stakeholder, to inform the design of technical specifications, and new developers rarely get to practice such conversations before facing real stakeholders. This paper explores whether an AI agent acting as a stakeholder can serve as a practice partner for requirements elicitation, with a focus on developing conversational skills, and producing quality user stories. The study aims to explore this with a systematic literature review, of (1) the skills and methods requirements elicitation involves and (2) how conversational AI has been used to train skills in other domains, and relate the findings to the few existing studies on AI stakeholder practice in requirements engineering. The review points to stakeholder realism, feedback and scenario diversity as central design concerns. Results analysis is still ongoing, so conclusions about feasibility are preliminary.
